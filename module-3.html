CQ Drive Why This Matters

Opening scenario: You are promoted to lead a mixed U.S.–India engineering team. At your first team meeting you announce a rotating decision-making model so everyone gets a turn to lead a sprint. Silence follows. The team nods. Two weeks later you discover the Indian team members are still defaulting to the most senior engineer for every decision. Why?

CQ Knowledge What You Need to Know

The GLOBE Framework

The GLOBE study (House et al., 2014) distinguishes between Culturally Endorsed Leadership Theories (CLTs) — what people believe ideal leaders should do — and Observed CEO Behavior — what leaders actually do. The gap between the two reveals cultural tensions and adaptation pressures.

India GLOBE Leadership Comparison (slide 15)

Dimension Culturally Endorsed (CLT) Observed CEO Behavior Gap
Charismatic 5.85 5.67 −0.18
Team-oriented 5.73 5.48 −0.25
Participative 4.99 4.74 −0.25
Self-protective 3.77 4.11 +0.34

Verbatim gap analysis (slide 15): Charismatic and team-oriented leadership are strongly endorsed. Participative behavior falls below the expected level. Self-protective behavior exceeds the endorsed ideal.

Engineering-Context Implications

Charismatic (5.85 / 5.67): Strong vision and direction work well. Lead with authority and credibility — both are expected and respected.

Team-oriented (5.73 / 5.48): Collaborative coordination is valued. Build in-group cohesion deliberately; it is a genuine strength to leverage.

Participative (4.99 / 4.74): Participation is endorsed but under-practiced. If you want input, actively create conditions for it — it won't surface spontaneously in a group setting with the authority figure present.

Self-protective (3.77 / 4.11): Face-saving and status protection exceed what is culturally ideal. Public challenges to authority damage working relationships even when well-intentioned. Use private channels. (House et al., 2014; Pennsylvania State University, n.d.-b)

Leadership Ethics Vignette (slide 17)

Situation: The local manager expects approval, but critical tooling measurements are out of specification.

Recommended sequence:

  1. VERIFY — Confirm measurements and acceptance criteria.
  2. DISCUSS — Meet privately and ask about supplier constraints.
  3. CO-DESIGN — Set owners, actions, evidence, and re-test dates.
  4. HOLD SIGN-OFF — Approve only after objective criteria are met.

Applied case from ENGR 802 vignette; ethics: National Society of Professional Engineers (2019), Canons I.1 and Rules II.1.b.

Watch out for overgeneralizing. India is regionally diverse. National Hofstede scores describe statistical tendencies across millions of people, not predictions about any individual. Treat these patterns as a starting point for observation, not a template for assumptions.

CQ Strategy Plan Ahead

GLOBE Pre-Interaction Checklist

  1. Am I inside or outside this team's in-group? (In-group = trusted inner circle; out-group = more formal, transactional)
  2. Which CLT dimension is active right now — charismatic, participative, or self-protective?
  3. Have I created a private channel for concerns before the group meeting?
  4. How do I move from out-group to in-group status? (Answer: invest in relationship time — ask about their work, their context, their constraints.)

CQ Action Do This, Not That

Do Avoid Why
Show humane orientation first: ask about workload, career goals, team constraints before diving into the technical agenda Leading with authority, process, or formal metrics before any relationship is established Humane orientation is the highest-endorsed leadership dimension in India — it is the trust signal that opens the door (House et al., 2014)
Introduce egalitarian or rotating structures gradually, starting within a trusted sub-team, after getting private buy-in from senior members Announcing a flat or rotating leadership model to a full hierarchy without prior consultation High PDI makes sudden role reversal socially disorienting, not empowering — it reads as undermining the existing order (Hofstede et al., 2010)

Apply It — Leadership Vignette Scenario

Situation: You are a U.S. engineer reviewing a tooling sign-off. The local Indian manager expects your approval today. Your measurements show critical tolerances are out of spec. The manager says the supplier has always delivered within acceptable range in practice. (Based on slide 17's ethics vignette)
Decision 1 What do you do?
Incorrect. Approving out-of-spec tooling violates NSPE Canon I.1 (public safety paramount). Cultural sensitivity does not override professional ethics — it shapes how you raise the concern, not whether you do. (National Society of Professional Engineers, 2019)
Correct. This follows the deck's recommended sequence: VERIFY → DISCUSS → CO-DESIGN → HOLD SIGN-OFF. The private meeting preserves face while keeping safety standards intact. (NSPE Canon I.1; slide 17)
Incorrect. Escalating over the manager's head without prior private conversation is a face-threatening act that damages trust and closes communication channels for the rest of the project. Try private discussion first. (Meyer, 2014; Hofstede et al., 2010)
Decision 2 In the private meeting, the manager shares supplier constraints you weren't aware of. What next?
Correct. This is the CO-DESIGN step from slide 17: joint ownership, documented evidence requirements, and a written re-test date. It addresses the technical issue while giving the manager an active role in the solution. (NSPE Rules II.1.b)
Incorrect. Extending the deadline without a written corrective action plan leaves the safety issue unresolved. Document the path to approval before any timeline change. (IEI, 2004; National Society of Professional Engineers, 2019)

Knowledge Checks

1. India's GLOBE Self-protective score (observed: 4.11) exceeding the endorsed CLT (3.77) most likely means:
The GLOBE gap analysis states: "Self-protective behavior exceeds the endorsed ideal." This means cultural pressure toward status protection and face-saving is higher in practice than the culture endorses as ideal — a signal to use private channels for sensitive conversations. (House et al., 2014)
The gap between endorsed CLT (3.77) and observed behavior (4.11) means self-protective behaviors occur more in practice than the culture considers ideal. It describes a structural pressure, not a character judgment. (House et al., 2014)
2. According to the deck's slide 17 vignette, in what order should a U.S. engineer handle a tooling sign-off dispute?
Verbatim from slide 17: VERIFY → DISCUSS (privately, ask about constraints) → CO-DESIGN (set owners, actions, evidence, re-test dates) → HOLD SIGN-OFF (approve only after objective criteria are met). (National Society of Professional Engineers, 2019, Canon I.1)
The correct sequence preserves face while maintaining safety standards. It begins with verification, moves to private discussion, then co-designed correction, and only approves when objective criteria are met. (slide 17)
3. India's GLOBE Participative score (endorsed: 4.99, observed: 4.74) suggests:
The gap (endorsed higher than observed) means participation is a valued but under-practiced norm. If you want input from an Indian team, create explicit channels for it: private check-ins, written recaps with corrections invited, one-on-one follow-ups. (House et al., 2014)
The endorsed score (4.99) is higher than observed (4.74), meaning participation is valued but under-practiced — it needs to be deliberately created, not assumed. (House et al., 2014)

Reflect

Think of a time you were treated as an out-group member — by a team, an organization, or a client. How did it affect your willingness to raise concerns or contribute ideas? What would have helped you move to in-group status faster?
