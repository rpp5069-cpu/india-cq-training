CQ Drive

Why This Matters

Scenario: You have been managing a highly productive Indian engineering team for six months. You decide to recognize the team by implementing a new "Individual Star Performer" bonus program. Within two weeks, team collaboration drops noticeably. The best-performing engineers start competing instead of helping each other. What happened?

The program design violated two overlapping cultural patterns: collectivism (IDV 48) makes individual competitive incentives feel divisive, and high PDI (77) means recognition through the authority chain carries more weight than peer recognition. This module gives you the evidence to design motivation approaches that actually work in India's IT and engineering context.

CQ Knowledge

What You Need to Know

Herzberg Two-Factor Theory in India

Herzberg two-factor theory applied to Indian IT engineering teams
Factor Type Examples in Indian IT Engineering Implication
Hygiene factors
(prevent dissatisfaction)		 Competitive salary, job security, safe working conditions, respectful management These do not motivate — they just prevent unhappiness. If salary feels unfair or the workplace feels unsafe, nothing else matters. Fix hygiene issues first. (Joseph et al., 2025)
Motivators
(drive engagement and performance)		 Achievement and recognition, interesting and challenging work, career advancement, responsibility These are the conditions that drive extra effort. India's IT sector respondents rate achievement and recognition highest among motivators. (Joseph et al., 2025; Gupta, 2020)

Joseph et al. (2025) — Key Finding

"Findings from the IT & ITES industry in India show that motivators such as achievement, recognition, and advancement drive retention. Hygiene factors alone — salary, job security — prevent dissatisfaction but do not create engagement. The practical implication: after fixing compensation issues, focus on creating meaningful work and visible career paths." (Joseph et al., 2025)

Nimmi & Jakubik (2023) — Workplace Wellbeing

  • Positive supervisor relationships are the strongest predictor of workplace wellbeing in Indian IT
  • Autonomy and meaningful work are second-tier drivers
  • Passive management (benign neglect) is linked to lower wellbeing even when hygiene factors are met
  • Practical implication: check in regularly, give meaningful challenges, and be specific when you recognize work (Nimmi & Jakubik, 2023)

Gupta (2020) — Leadership, Motivation, and Innovation

  • Leader–member exchange (LMX) quality significantly predicts employee innovation in India
  • Employees in strong LMX relationships are more likely to propose new solutions
  • Practical implication: invest time in one-on-one relationships, not just team-level interactions; individual relationship quality drives innovation (Gupta, 2020)

PDI Context for Recognition

In high-PDI (77) cultures, public recognition is powerful but must be managed carefully: (Hofstede et al., 2010)

  • Group recognition is generally safer than singling out individuals in front of peers
  • Private recognition (direct, specific feedback in a 1:1 setting) is universally effective
  • Recognition of technical contribution by a senior authority figure carries more weight than peer recognition
  • Competitive individual incentives can undermine collectivist team cohesion

Watch out for overgeneralizing. India is regionally diverse. National Hofstede scores describe statistical tendencies across millions of people, not predictions about any individual. Treat these patterns as a starting point for observation, not a template for assumptions.

CQ Strategy

Plan Ahead — Three Questions Before You Motivate

Pre-Motivation Checklist
1
What hygiene issues exist right now?

Salary equity, job security, safe environment. Fix these first — motivators cannot work if basic needs are unmet. (Joseph et al., 2025)

2
What work is this person most engaged with?

Ask directly in a private 1:1: "What work has felt most meaningful in the last month?" Achievement is the top driver — make sure the assignment matches their skills. (Gupta, 2020)

3
What recognition has been given recently, and was it public or private?

In PDI 77 culture, the most credible recognition comes from the most senior person who can plausibly comment on the work — delivered specifically and privately. (Hofstede et al., 2010; Nimmi & Jakubik, 2023)

1 Joseph et al. (2025) found that in India's IT sector, the strongest driver of employee engagement (beyond preventing dissatisfaction) is:
Correct. Joseph et al. (2025) found that motivators — achievement, recognition, and career advancement — are the primary drivers of engagement in India's IT sector. Hygiene factors (salary, job security) only prevent dissatisfaction; they don't generate engagement. (Joseph et al., 2025)
Salary, security, and clear roles are hygiene factors — they prevent dissatisfaction but do not create engagement. The drivers of engagement are motivators: achievement, recognition, meaningful work, and advancement. (Joseph et al., 2025)
2 Nimmi & Jakubik (2023) found that the strongest predictor of workplace wellbeing in Indian IT engineers is:
Correct. Nimmi & Jakubik (2023) found that positive supervisor relationships are the strongest predictor of workplace wellbeing — above autonomy, tools, or even compensation. This means weekly 1:1s with genuine engagement are one of the highest-ROI management activities. (Nimmi & Jakubik, 2023)
Compensation and tools are hygiene factors. Nimmi & Jakubik (2023) found that the supervisor relationship quality is the most predictive factor — which shifts the management focus from systems to interactions. (Nimmi & Jakubik, 2023)
3 When combining Hofstede's PDI score for India (77) with Herzberg's theory, which recognition approach is most effective?
Correct. High PDI means authority-endorsed recognition carries the most weight — peer elections and public leaderboards are both muted in collectivist, high-PDI cultures. The highest-impact recognition is specific, private, and comes from a senior leader. (Hofstede et al., 2010)
