CQ Drive Why This Matters

Opening scenario: Before your flight lands in Pune, you have six hours. What cultural assumptions are you bringing that could backfire on Day 1? This module uses your team's own self-reflections as worked examples — then asks you to build your own.

Caroline Auer Module 1 lead

Cultural observations (slide 19): Ever-changing, fast-paced environment allows for a flexible mindset. High respect for authority and status ingrained at a young age. Image preserving is highly prioritized, along with interpersonal harmony.

India expectation U.S. default assumption to check
"Timing, specifications, and deadlines are flexible" "Shifting timelines or informal documentation could feel chaotic or unprofessional"

Wenkai Hu Modules 3 & 6 lead

Common assumptions U.S.-trained engineers should reconsider (slide 20):

  • "Direct disagreement in meetings is always the most effective way to raise technical concerns."
  • "Silence or a lack of objections means everyone agrees."
  • "Individual accountability and recognition work equally well across cultures."

Personal CQ reflection:

  • Strength: CQ Strategy (90th percentile) and Planning (98th percentile).
  • Behavioral preference: Strong emphasis on long-term planning, predictability, and achievement.
  • Potential blind spot: Assuming that a well-defined technical plan automatically leads to effective team coordination.
  • Adaptation: Understand local decision-making expectations, invite feedback through appropriate channels, and verify shared understanding before execution.
  • Key takeaway: "Effective cross-cultural collaboration requires adapting how we communicate and coordinate — not abandoning technical standards."
Rickey Patel Module 7 lead

"My Evidence-First Instincts Collide with India's Authority-Gated Innovation Culture" (slide 21)

Engineering defaults:

  • Data-first orientation: I build the evidence and expect it to persuade — my research default.
  • Direct communicator: trained to surface problems openly in group settings.
  • Heritage complication: Gujarati roots create a sense of cultural fluency that hasn't been professionally tested in India's engineering context.

Where it creates friction:

  • PDI at 77 means adoption requires an authority champion — not just a compelling dataset.
  • My instinct to present evidence to the group before briefing the senior lead privately violates the high-PDI approval sequence.
  • Moderate CQ Drive: heritage familiarity can suppress motivation to prepare deliberately.

CQ adjustment:

  • CQ Strategy (strong): Apply my own Section 7 three-step rule — brief the authority lead privately 24–48 hrs before any group presentation.
  • CQ Action (strong): Adapt framing to position contributions as a local advantage, not a foreign recommendation.
  • One rule: "the evidence never goes into the room cold."

Insight: "My biggest CQ risk isn't unfamiliarity with India — it's misplaced familiarity that skips deliberate preparation." (Hofstede Insights, 2026; Rogers, 2003)

Christopher Toevs Module 5 lead

"How might my working style affect what colleagues share?" (slide 22)

Preference Assumption to check Adaptation to try
Open debate I expect disagreement to surface in meetings. Check in privately before interpreting silence as agreement.
Direct feedback I expect blunt feedback to help people improve quickly. Ask how colleagues prefer to receive sensitive feedback.
Independent decisions I expect going directly to a specialist to save time. Ask local leads who should be consulted before deciding.

On the team Mark worked with in India, more one-on-ones and greater involvement from local leads improved information flow. (Mark, personal communication, November 14, 2026)

Aureo Zanon Module 2 lead

"High CQ Skills, but My Doing Drive Can Outrun Trust in India" (slide 23)

CQ Profile: Overall CQ 83; Knowledge, Strategy, and Action all at 88. Strengths: Sociolinguistics 99 and Speech Acts 96. Gaps: CQ Drive 69, Self-Efficacy 60, Values & Norms 71. Preferences: strong Doing and Collectivist; mid on Power Distance.

Where it creates friction:

  • Doing vs. relationship-first: I can push for results before trust is built.
  • PDI 77: on an Agentic AI pilot, my India team stayed quiet on the timeline. I took it as agreement; the slip came weeks later.
  • Self-Efficacy 60: I may hold back or let others lead, even when I have the skills.

CQ adjustment:

  • CQ Strategy: check silence one-on-one or in writing before I read it as a yes.
  • CQ Action: open meetings with relationship time before tasks and deadlines.
  • CQ Drive: log cross-cultural wins weekly and volunteer to lead, so confidence rests on evidence.

Insight: "My skills to adapt are strong. The risk is letting my Doing drive move faster than trust and checking." (CQ Pro Report, self-rating (Cultural Intelligence Center, 2026); Hofstede et al., 2010; Presenter's experience with India-based teams)
